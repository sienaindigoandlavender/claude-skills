# Slow Morocco Voice

The premise is **Morocco, Decoded**. The country is illegible to most readers. The work is taking what's opaque and showing what it actually is. Every piece of writing serves that decoding.

This skill defines the voice, format, and discipline that makes Slow Morocco's writing recognizable across all its forms — wiki entries, book chapters, free essays, the letter, app text, and any other reader-facing prose.

---

## The Core Shift: Gossip, Not Lessons

The single most important register decision: **Slow Morocco writes gossip, not lessons.** Gossip is specific, scene-driven, fact-dense, slightly impolite, alive with named people and exact numbers. Lessons are general, thesis-driven, abstract, dutifully neutral, full of "important" and "rich tradition."

Gossip earns the reader's attention. Lessons assume it.

| Lessons (avoid) | Gossip (use) |
|---|---|
| "Morocco has a rich tradition of textile production..." | "The carpet merchant will tell you it's antique. It isn't." |
| "The medina is a UNESCO World Heritage site..." | "Bill Willis arrived for a Getty wedding in 1966 and never left." |
| "Traditional crafts are an important part..." | "Fifty thousand Moroccans earn their living selling trilobites." |
| "Moroccan cuisine is renowned for..." | "Couscous is Amazigh. The Arabs adopted it later." |
| "The Almohads were a powerful dynasty..." | "The Almohads tore down what the Almoravids built. Then the Marinids tore down theirs." |

When in doubt, ask: *would a smart friend at a dinner party say this, or only a museum placard?*

## The Formula

Every piece of writing follows this structure, whether it's a 200-word wiki entry or a 1,200-word essay:

1. **Tableau opening** — Drop into a scene, a fact, a specific moment. Not a thesis. Not throat-clearing.
2. **Specific facts** — Names, dates, prices, numbers, places. Every paragraph carries at least one concrete detail.
3. **The pivot** — A turn. "Then came..." / "The irony is..." / "What remains is..." / "Until..."
4. **Tidbits as moat** — The weird, the specific, the detail nobody else has. These make the writing defensible.

---

## Opening Lines

The opening is the contract with the reader. It must do work in the first sentence.

### Good openings

- "The trilobite looks like a stone horseshoe crab, curled into a defensive ball."
- "They arrive before dawn, when the desert air is still cold."
- "The word means 'salt' in Arabic."
- "Six thousand years ago, you could have canoed across the Sahara."
- "The fennec fox has ears the size of its face."
- "Bill Willis arrived for a Getty wedding in 1966 and never left."
- "Couscous is Amazigh. The Arabs adopted it later."
- "The Almohads tore down what the Almoravids built."

What makes them work: a specific image, an unexpected fact, a direct claim, no preamble.

### Bad openings (banned)

- "Morocco has a long history of..."
- "The Sahara is the world's largest..."
- "Traditional Moroccan culture..."
- "Throughout history, Morocco has..."
- "When most people think of Morocco..."
- "In this entry/essay, we will explore..."
- "It is important to understand..."

What makes them fail: throat-clearing, generality, writer's discomfort showing, no fact, no scene, no claim.

## Tidbits as Moat

What makes Slow Morocco's writing defensible against AI summary, against tourist content, against parrot guides: **the specific details nobody else has.**

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Documentary wins | "Tinghir-Jerusalem won Best New Director at Tangier Film Festival" |
| Celebrity connections | "Bill Willis designed for the Gettys (3x), Rothschilds, Agnellis, YSL" |
| Prices | "A good riding camel fetches thousands of dirhams" |
| Industry stats | "50,000+ Moroccans work in the fossil trade — $40M/year" |
| Weird facts | "Tuareg blue comes from pounding, not boiling — saves water, stains skin" |
| Modern echoes | "The buyers increasingly arrive in 4x4s rather than on camelback" |
| Scientific specifics | "Trilobite eyes made of calcite — earliest known visual systems" |
| Historical dates | "First mellah: Fez, 1438. Jews had been there 2,000 years already." |
| Linguistic precision | "The word means 'salt' in Arabic — and the Sahara was paid for in it." |
| Architectural specifics | "Bou Inania's mihrab points 8 degrees off true qibla. Nobody knows why." |

Every entry should contain at least 2 of these. They're the moat. AI summaries can compress your prose; they can't reproduce the proprietary specifics.

## The Pivot

Every entry has a turn. The reader is led somewhere, then the writing shifts. This is what keeps prose alive across a 1-2 page entry. Without a pivot, the writing flatlines.

Pivot phrases that work:

- "Then came..."
- "Until..."
- "The irony is..."
- "What remains is..."
- "But the truth is..."
- "Three centuries later..."
- "The British took notice."
- "By 1956 it was over."

The pivot doesn't announce itself. It happens. The reader feels the turn without being told.

## Format

### Length

- **Wiki entries**: 1-2 pages (300-700 words)
- **Book entries**: 1-2 pages (300-700 words)
- **App entries**: same — same content, different presentation
- **Free essays**: 600-1,200 words (longer atmospheric pieces allowed when scene-building benefits)
- **Letter dispatches**: variable, but composed of entries plus framing

### Structure within a wiki/book entry

A consistent shape that holds across all entries:

```
# [Title]

[Brief context line — date range, location, type]

## What It Was / What It Is

[2-4 sentences. Tableau opening. Specific. Concrete.]

## Why It Matters

[2-4 sentences. The downstream consequences. The connections.]

## What Survives / What Remains

[2-4 sentences. What's still visible, audible, edible, walkable today. The line back from history to now.]

## Reading Further

[Cross-references using [[wiki-link]] syntax. 3-6 links to related entries.]

## Sources

[1-3 authoritative sources. Even private notes benefit.]
```

This is the default. Some entries will deviate — a profile of a person might use a different shape; a definition entry might be even shorter. Default to the shape; deviate when the material demands it.

### Cross-linking

Be generous. Every reference to another period, dynasty, building, person, place, plant, dish, ceremony, term that has its own entry should link to it using `[[wiki-link]]` syntax.

The linked entry might not exist yet. Link anyway. The broken link becomes the to-do list.

## Tense

- **Present tense for ongoing things**: "The market persists." "The maâlem still works in his Marrakech atelier."
- **Past tense for completed things**: "He died in 2009." "The dynasty fell in 1465."
- **No historical present** for events long completed. "Ibn Battuta departs Tangier in 1325" reads as journalist-affectation. Use "Ibn Battuta left Tangier in 1325."

---

## Source Discipline

Every claim needs a receipt. Even in private wiki entries, cite the source.

Source types:

| Type | Use for |
|---|---|
| **Historical** | Documented events, dates, names |
| **Archaeological** | Physical evidence, excavations |
| **Scientific** | Peer-reviewed, measurable claims |
| **Ethnographic** | Living practice, interviews |
| **Literary** | Books, films, documented texts |

When uncertain, mark it: `[uncertain — verify]`. Do not fudge. The wiki is a thinking tool; lying in it pollutes the thinking.

## The Toll Booth Discipline

Slow Morocco gives away the *concept* and *atmosphere*. It withholds the *operational detail*.

In any free piece of writing:

- ✅ Describe what a real lila is, what the seven mluk are, what the trance does
- ❌ Don't name the maâlem who runs the real ones
- ✅ Describe what an authentic carpet is and how to recognize it
- ❌ Don't name the specific vendor in Marrakech
- ✅ Describe how Saharan navigation works, what the sky-readers know
- ❌ Don't publish the location of the rock art

A free essay should leave the reader *feeling the existence of more*, not *fully served*. If they feel fully served, the essay overshared. Rewrite.

This discipline applies to wiki entries marked `publishable` (intended for downstream public use) but is more relaxed in entries marked `private` or `internal_draft`. The wiki is for thinking; the published version is for readers.

## What Slow Morocco Never Writes

- **"Morocco has a rich tradition of..."** — banned phrase. Always rewrite.
- **"The last of..."** — elegy framing banned. Even when something is genuinely disappearing, frame it as continuing struggle, not nostalgic loss.
- **First person** — unless quoting, or unless the form explicitly calls for it (a letter dispatch, a personal note). The default voice is third-person editorial.
- **Tourist tips** — this is not a guidebook. No "best time to visit" or "what to pack."
- **Generic descriptions** — "beautiful," "authentic," "magical," "magnificent" all banned. Use specific descriptions or no descriptions.
- **Wellness register** — no "discover your inner journey," no "transformation," no "step into your power." The register is comprehension, not transformation.
- **Snake-charmer warmth** — no "habibti," no manufactured intimacy, no over-promising magic. The work is real; the prose is steady.
- **Throat-clearing openings** — "Today I want to discuss..." "It's important to consider..." "When we think about..."
- **"In conclusion"** — entries end when the decoding is done. No wrap-up paragraphs.

## The Amazigh Substrate

Slow Morocco's editorial position is that **the Amazigh substrate runs underneath all of Moroccan history and culture**, and most mainstream writing about Morocco erases it. The voice corrects this without becoming activist.

Practical consequences:

- When introducing the Almoravids: "the Almoravids were a Berber Sanhaja confederation," not "the Almoravids were a Muslim dynasty"
- When writing about pre-Islamic Morocco: lead with the Amazigh world, not the Roman colonial layer
- When writing about food: couscous is Amazigh; msemen is Amazigh; amlou is Amazigh — they became "Moroccan" later
- When writing about Sufism: the marabout/saint tradition is fundamentally Berber; Sufism layered on top
- When writing about language: name Tamazight, Tashelhit, Tarifit, Hassaniya as distinct realities, not as folkloric variants of "Berber"

This is editorial position, not just chronology. Hold it consistently.

## Photography Direction (when relevant)

When photographs accompany text, the visual register matches the prose register: documentary, restrained, specific.

Standard prompt for AI image generation (Midjourney):

```
documentary editorial photography, muted but luminous color,
soft natural light, open shadows, no crushed blacks, low contrast,
desaturated but true color, dusty earth tones, sky remains blue,
timeless atmosphere, quiet composition, negative space,
single clear subject, realistic texture, unstyled and honest,
film photography look, medium format camera, 35mm lens,
f/2.8, ISO 200, grain 8%, not cinematic, not dramatic
--style raw --s 70 --chaos 8 --ar 16:9
```

Scene-specific additions:

- **Market**: "crowded but single focal point, morning light, dust rising"
- **Desert**: "vast negative space, single human figure for scale"
- **Interior**: "filtered light through high windows, architectural geometry"
- **Craft**: "hands working, shallow depth of field, intimate scale"
- **Wildlife**: "documentary style, natural behavior, dawn or dusk light"

Never use place names in prompts (returns generic stock results). Describe the *kind* of scene.

## Quality Check

Before completing any piece of writing, run through:

- [ ] Tableau opening — scene, not thesis?
- [ ] At least 5 specific facts (numbers, dates, names, prices, places)?
- [ ] At least 2 "moat" tidbits (weird, specific, defensible)?
- [ ] Sources cited (or marked uncertain)?
- [ ] No elegy framing ("the last of...")?
- [ ] No generic descriptions ("beautiful," "magical," "rich tradition")?
- [ ] No throat-clearing opening or "in conclusion" close?
- [ ] Cross-references to other entries via `[[wiki-link]]`?
- [ ] Amazigh substrate honored where relevant?
- [ ] Pivot present — does the writing turn?
- [ ] Reader left wanting more, not feeling fully served?

If any answer is no, rewrite the piece.

## Examples of Voice in Action

### Wiki entry opening (good)

> The Almoravids were a Berber Sanhaja confederation that rose from the western Sahara in the 1040s. Within fifty years they ruled from the Senegal River to the Ebro. They built Marrakech in 1062. They gave their name to the architectural austerity that would dominate Maghrebi religious building for the next century. Then they were destroyed by their own students.

What works: Specific (1040s, 1062, Senegal River, Ebro). Concrete claim (built Marrakech). Pivot (then they were destroyed). The phrase "their own students" is a hook that pulls the reader to the next paragraph about the Almohads.

### Wiki entry opening (bad — rewritten from the good above)

> The Almoravids were one of the most important dynasties in Moroccan history. They had a significant impact on the development of Moroccan architecture and culture. Their reign saw the founding of Marrakech and the expansion of their empire across North Africa and into Spain.

What fails: Generic ("most important," "significant impact," "development"). No specific dates beyond "their reign." No pivot. No moat. Indistinguishable from any encyclopedia entry. AI could produce this.

### Free essay opening (good)

> The carpet merchant will tell you it's antique. It isn't. The dyes are aniline, the wool is machine-spun, the pattern is from a book Berbers don't own. He learned the speech from his father, who learned it from a French dealer in 1962. The carpet is forty years old. Some of them are good carpets anyway.

What works: Direct address to the reader's likely experience. Specific facts (1962, "machine-spun," "aniline"). Pivot at the end ("Some of them are good carpets anyway"). Voice — slightly impolite, knowing, not preachy.
